SECTION 1: EXPLAINING PROMPT ENGINEERING


What is Prompt Engineering?

Prompt engineering is simply the practice of writing clear, specific instructions for AI tools. Think of it like giving directions—the better your directions, the better results you get. Instead of vague instructions, you provide context, examples, and clear expectations so the AI understands exactly what you want.

Why Does It Matter?

When using AI tools like Claude, the quality of your output depends directly on the quality of your input. Good prompts save you time because:

You get better answers faster — no need to ask follow-up questions or rephrase
Results are more useful — the AI understands your specific needs and constraints
Less editing required — well-prompted responses often need minimal adjustments

It's the difference between asking "Write something" and "Write a professional email asking for a meeting, keeping it under 100 words, using a friendly tone."

SECTION 2: WEAK VS IMPROVED PROMPT EXAMPLE


❌ Weak Prompt

Write about climate change

Output you might get:

"Climate change is when Earth gets warmer. It happens because of greenhouse gases. This is bad for nature."
(Generic, surface-level, unclear what you need)

✅ Improved Prompt

Write a 150-word explanation of climate change for a high school student. 
Focus on the causes (greenhouse gases) and one specific impact (rising sea levels). 
Use simple language and one real example. Format as 2-3 paragraphs.

Output you'll get:

A clear, well-structured explanation tailored to the exact audience and length you need, with a specific impact and real example.

Three Practical Benefits of Better Prompts

Specificity saves time — You define exactly what you want (length, tone, format) upfront instead of iterating multiple times
Context improves quality — Telling Claude who you're writing for, what problem you're solving, or what style you want produces far more relevant results
Constraints prevent waste — Specifying limits (word count, specific topics, tone) keeps responses focused and usable immediately
